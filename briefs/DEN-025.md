## 5. Direct-answer target

Open with the evidence limitation and useful bundled number, not a publisher name:

**Current U.S. cost research does not provide a clean exam-only national range. The strongest current published benchmark reports a $203 average and a $50–$350 range for a routine dental exam that includes a full cleaning and X-rays. Because the source prices a bundle, those figures should not be presented as the cost of an exam alone.**

Immediately after the answer, attribute the benchmark to CareCredit/Synchrony and state the 2023–2024 research / 50-states-plus-DC scope. Explain that a written visit estimate should identify the evaluation/visit category and whether cleaning and imaging are included, separate or unclear.

Editorial attribution rule: **answer first, source second**. Do not rotate publishers for cosmetic variety.

### H2 — What kind of dental visit is on the quote?
- User question: Why do dental visit labels differ?
- Direct answer: periodic, comprehensive, limited/problem-focused, periodontal-evaluation, consultation and after-hours visit categories are not interchangeable service labels.
- Entity/attributes: visit category, quote label, service scope
- Evidence: DVC-002, DVC-003, DVC-005, DVC-006
- Format: compact category table

#### H3 — Periodic and comprehensive evaluations
- Direct answer: these are distinct evaluation categories; the page may explain the label but must not tell a reader which one they need.
- Evidence: DVC-003

#### H3 — Problem-focused, consultation and after-hours visits
- Direct answer: these can be separately classified services; do not assign the routine bundle price to them unless the written quote has the same scope.
- Evidence: DVC-005, DVC-006

### H2 — What should a dentist-visit quote identify?
- User question: What should I check before comparing two dentist-visit quotes?
- Direct answer: confirm the service/evaluation label, whether cleaning and X-rays are included, whether other charges are separate, and whether the amount is a provider fee or an insurance-plan member amount.
- Entity/attributes: category, included services, price type, quote unit
- Evidence: DVC-001, DVC-005–007 + evidence rules
- Format: checklist or compact table

### H2 — Are cleaning and X-rays included in a dental checkup price?
- User question: Does a checkup price automatically include cleaning or imaging?
- Direct answer: not universally. The CareCredit benchmark is a specific bundle and Delta notes that routine visits may combine exam, cleaning and X-ray review/taking; written quotes must identify what is actually included.
- Entity/attributes: cleaning inclusion, imaging inclusion, bundle scope
- Evidence: DVC-001, DVC-004
- Format: answer + included/separate/unknown framework

### H2 — What can change a dentist-visit quote?
- User question: Why can two visit quotes differ?
- Direct answer: supported distinctions include visit category, included cleaning/imaging, geography/provider pricing, network status and plan-specific cost sharing; unsupported multipliers must not be invented.
- Entity/attributes: service category, bundle components, geography/provider, insurance
- Evidence: DVC-001, DVC-005–007
- Format: concise driver list with evidence limits

### H2 — How insurance can affect what you pay for a dental visit
- User question: How much does insurance pay for a dental exam?
- Direct answer: there is no universal coverage percentage, copay or exam frequency. Plans can classify periodic, comprehensive, problem-focused and consultation services differently and apply their own network/frequency/cost-sharing rules.
- Entity/attributes: insurance mechanics, patient responsibility, visit category
- Evidence: DVC-005–007; GUI-001 owns full insurance explanation
- Format: short answer + bridge to GUI-001
