## 5. Direct-answer target

Open with the evidence limitation and useful number, not the publisher name:

**Current U.S. cost research does not provide a clean onlay-only national range. The strongest recent study reports a $976 average and a $755–$1,774 range for a single ceramic inlay or onlay combined. Because the study groups the two restorations together, those figures should not be presented as an onlay-only national price range.**

Immediately after the answer, attribute the benchmark to CareCredit/Synchrony and state the 2024 study / 50-states-plus-DC scope. Explain that an individual written onlay quote still needs its material, surface count, quoted unit and inclusion status checked.

Editorial attribution rule: **answer first, source second**. Do not rotate publishers for cosmetic variety.

- User question: What does an onlay cost in the U.S.?

### H2 — Why the current national price data combines onlays and inlays
- User question: Why can’t the published range be called an onlay-only range?
- Direct answer: the strongest current cost study uses one combined ceramic inlay-or-onlay category and does not report separate national values for the two restorations.
- Entity/attributes: source category, scope limitation, onlay vs inlay
- Evidence: ONL-001, ONL-002
- Format: short explanation + methodology note

### H2 — What makes an onlay a distinct restoration?
- User question: Why does this page exist separately from the inlay, filling and crown pages?
- Direct answer: ADA terminology defines an onlay as an indirect restoration covering one or more cusp tips and adjoining chewing surfaces without covering the whole external tooth surface.
- Entity/attributes: onlay definition, architecture boundary
- Evidence: ONL-004
- Format: brief definition tied only to cost-page ownership, not treatment choice

### H2 — What should an onlay quote identify?
- User question: What information do I need before comparing two onlay quotes?
- Direct answer: confirm the material, surface count, quoted unit, and whether related services are included, separate or unclear.
- Entity/attributes: material, surfaces, unit, inclusions
- Evidence: ONL-005, ONL-006, ONL-007 + evidence rules
- Format: checklist or compact table

#### H3 — Material on the quote
- Direct answer: indirect onlay materials may be described as metallic, porcelain/ceramic or resin-based composite; the page may record the label but must not recommend a material.
- Evidence: ONL-005, ONL-007

#### H3 — Surface count on the quote
- Direct answer: the named 2026 plan distinguishes metallic onlays by two, three and four-or-more surfaces; do not infer surface count from price.
- Evidence: ONL-006

### H2 — Are laboratory and related service costs included in an onlay price?
- User question: Does an onlay price automatically include laboratory work, imaging or other related services?
- Direct answer: current price evidence does not establish a universal inclusion rule; the written quote must show whether related services are included, separate, not listed or unclear.
- Entity/attributes: inclusion status, lab work, imaging, temporary restoration, cementation
- Evidence: evidence-packet inclusion restriction
- Format: answer + included/separate/unknown framework

### H2 — What can change an onlay quote?
- User question: Why can two onlay quotes differ?
- Direct answer: the strongest supported quote distinctions are material, surface count, provider/geographic pricing and what the quote includes; unsupported adjustment percentages must not be invented.
- Entity/attributes: material, surface count, provider/geography, inclusions
- Evidence: ONL-005–007 + price-scope rules
- Format: concise driver list with evidence limits

### H2 — Onlay repair is not the same price category as a new onlay
- User question: Can an onlay-repair price be used as the cost of a new onlay?
