## 1. Evidence rules for GUI-002

1. GUI-002 owns lower-cost/no-insurance access guidance, not procedure-specific price benchmarks.
2. Do not claim a specific clinic, school, charity or health center is available to a particular reader without live local verification.
3. Do not promise free care, a specific discount, appointment availability, treatment eligibility or financial-assistance eligibility.
4. Official locator tools and program categories may be described; the reader must verify current services, eligibility, wait times, scope and charges directly with the organization.
5. Procedure-specific prices remain on procedure owners. Insurance benefit mechanics remain GUI-001. Insurance premium shopping remains deferred DEF-037.
6. No diagnosis, treatment selection or recommendation that a particular program is clinically appropriate.

## 2. Primary federal access source

### ADC-001 — National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research: Finding Dental Care
URL: https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/finding-dental-care  
Publisher: NIH / National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research  
Last reviewed on page: December 2024  
Use: authoritative directory of lower-cost care pathways

Verified statements:
- NIDCR says it does not provide dental care or financial assistance.
- For lower-cost dental care, NIDCR recommends asking dental schools about their clinic services.
- Dental schools often have clinics where students provide patient care at reduced cost under close supervision by experienced, licensed dentists.
- Dental hygiene schools may offer supervised, low-cost preventive dental care.
- Federally funded community health centers may offer care where the amount paid can depend on income.
- State or local health departments may know of free or reduced-cost programs in the area.
- NIDCR recommends 2-1-1 as another route to find local services.
- NIDCR notes that clinical trials may sometimes provide limited free or low-cost treatment for the condition being studied; this is study-specific and not general dental assistance.

Publication restrictions:
- “Often,” “may,” and “can” must remain conditional.
- Do not turn NIDCR's resource categories into guaranteed local availability.
- Do not imply NIDCR itself pays for dental treatment.

## 3. Federally funded health centers and sliding fees

### ADC-002 — HRSA Bureau of Primary Health Care: Health Center Program Compliance FAQs
URL: https://bphc.hrsa.gov/compliance/health-center-program-compliance-faqs  
Publisher: Health Resources and Services Administration  
Use: sliding-fee mechanics; no procedure-price benchmark

Verified statements:
- Health centers can use separate sliding-fee discount schedules for broad service types such as medical and dental.
- Eligibility for sliding-fee discounts, including any nominal charges, is based solely on income and family size under the Health Center Program requirements described by HRSA.
- A nominal charge is not a minimum fee, minimum charge or copay.
- Actual amounts can vary by the health center's fee schedule and service type.

Publication-safe implication:
- GUI-002 may tell readers to ask a participating health center whether dental services are offered and whether a sliding-fee schedule applies to the dental service they need.

Blocked inference:
- Do not claim that every health center offers every dental procedure.
- Do not publish a universal sliding-fee percentage or dollar amount.

### ADC-003 — HRSA Find a Health Center
Locator referenced by NIDCR: https://findahealthcenter.hrsa.gov/  
Use: official locator pathway only

Restriction:
- The site may link to the locator but must not claim a result is nearby, accepting patients, or offering a specific procedure without current verification.

## 5. Medicaid and CHIP

### ADC-005 — Medicaid.gov: Dental Care
URL: https://www.medicaid.gov/medicaid/benefits/dental-care  
Publisher: Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services  
Use: federal/state coverage distinction

Verified statements:
- States are required to provide dental benefits to children enrolled in Medicaid and CHIP under the applicable federal frameworks.
- Adult Medicaid dental benefits are determined by states; there is no federal minimum adult dental benefit requirement.
- States must make participating Medicaid/CHIP dental-provider information available through applicable program resources.

Publication-safe implication:
- GUI-002 may advise a reader who is enrolled or potentially eligible to check the current state Medicaid or CHIP program and participating-provider information.

Restrictions:
- Do not determine eligibility.
- Do not claim a specific adult dental service is covered in every state.
- Do not state a fixed copay, annual limit or covered-frequency rule without state/plan evidence.

### ADC-006 — Medicaid.gov: CHIP Benefits
URL: https://www.medicaid.gov/chip/benefits  
Use: corroborates required dental benefit in separate CHIP programs and state variation in program design.

## 7. GUI-002 approved evidence conclusions

SUPPORTED:
- Dental schools and dental-hygiene schools are legitimate pathways to ask about supervised reduced-cost care.
- Federally funded health centers are legitimate places to ask about dental services and income/family-size-based sliding-fee discounts.
- State/local health departments and 2-1-1 are legitimate routes for finding community programs.
- Medicaid dental coverage for children is required within federal program rules; adult Medicaid dental benefits vary by state.
- CHIP includes required dental benefits within applicable program structures.
- Readers should verify service scope, fees, eligibility, wait times and appointment availability directly with each program or clinic.

UNSUPPORTED / BLOCKED:
- A national “cheap dentist” price.
- A guaranteed discount percentage or free-care promise.
- A claim that a specific clinic, school or health center currently offers a particular procedure.
- A claim that a specific reader qualifies for Medicaid, CHIP, a sliding-fee schedule, charity care or a clinical trial.
- A national directory of local clinics maintained by this site.
- Treatment recommendations or diagnosis.

## 8. First-version page direction

GUI-002 should be an **access-verification guide, not a price calculator or local directory**.

Recommended answer-first framing:
> Legitimate lower-cost dental-care pathways in the U.S. include dental and dental-hygiene school clinics, federally funded health centers, Medicaid/CHIP where applicable, and state or local assistance resources. Availability, fees, eligibility and services vary, so verify each option directly before relying on it.

The page should give readers a short verification checklist:
- Does this location provide the dental service I need?
- Is the quoted amount a full fee, sliding-fee amount or estimated patient balance?
- What eligibility documents are required?
- Are X-rays, exams, lab work or follow-up visits included?
- Is there a wait list or referral requirement?
- If insurance is involved, is the provider in network?

This evidence packet is sufficient for a conservative first version of GUI-002.
