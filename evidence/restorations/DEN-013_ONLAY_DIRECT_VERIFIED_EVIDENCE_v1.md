## 1. Evidence rules for DEN-013

1. DEN-013 owns dental onlay cost. DEN-004 owns dental inlay cost. Keep the two URLs separate even when a price source groups them together.
2. Preserve the exact priced unit: onlay vs. inlay, material, surface count, geography, and whether a figure is a market observation or plan-specific member cost.
3. Do not relabel a combined `ceramic inlay or onlay` price as an onlay-only national benchmark.
4. Do not convert plan copays, plan allowances, local fee schedules, or one provider's price into a U.S.-wide cash-price estimate.
5. No material recommendation, treatment selection, diagnosis, eligibility decision, or claim that a reader needs an onlay rather than an inlay, filling, or crown.
6. Insurance coverage is plan-specific. Do not apply a default coverage percentage, deductible, annual maximum, frequency limit, alternate benefit, or network rule.
7. If a source does not state whether an exam, X-ray, temporary restoration, lab charge, cementation, repair, recementing, or another service is included, treat inclusion as unknown.

- Reported combined range: **$755–$1,774**.

Critical scope restriction:
- The source explicitly combines ceramic inlays and onlays in one priced category.

Publication-safe wording:
> A current U.S. cost study reports a $976 average and $755–$1,774 range for a single ceramic **inlay or onlay**. The source does not separate the two restorations, so those figures should not be treated as an onlay-only national range.

## 4. Clinical terminology / scope evidence

### ONL-004 — American Dental Association: Glossary of Dental Terms
URL: https://www.ada.org/publications/cdt/glossary-dental-terms  
Publisher: American Dental Association  
Use: onlay definition / architecture boundary; no price data

Verified definition usable for cost education:
- An onlay is a dental restoration made outside the oral cavity that covers one or more cusp tips and adjoining occlusal surfaces, but not the entire external surface, and is retained by luting cement.

Architecture implication:
- This supports treating an onlay as a distinct restoration entity rather than merely a keyword synonym for inlay, filling, or crown.

Safety boundary:
- The definition does not determine whether a particular reader needs an onlay.

### ONL-005 — American Dental Association: Materials for Indirect Restorations
URL: https://www.ada.org/resources/ada-library/oral-health-topics/materials-for-indirect-restorations  
Publisher: American Dental Association  
Use: material-category context only; no site price multiplier

Verified scope usable here:
- Indirect restorative materials used for inlays/onlays can include high-noble/noble alloys, all-ceramic materials, and resin-based composites.
- Material properties vary, but this page must not turn those differences into a treatment recommendation or an unsupported cost adjustment.

## 6. DEN-013 approved evidence conclusions

SUPPORTED:
- Onlay is a distinct restoration entity and site owner separate from inlay, filling and crown pages.
- A current national CareCredit/Synchrony study supports **$976 average / $755–$1,774 range for a single ceramic inlay or onlay combined**.
- The strongest current source does **not** provide a clean onlay-only national benchmark.
- Material and surface count are legitimate quote/service descriptors.
- Metallic onlays are separately coded for two, three, and four-or-more surfaces in the named 2026 Humana FEDVIP plan.
- Onlay repair is a separate service category from placement of a new onlay.
- Insurance handling can depend on plan-specific deductible, network, cost share, annual maximum, frequency limits and alternate-benefit rules.

UNSUPPORTED / BLOCKED:
- A current high-confidence **onlay-only** national range derived from ONL-001/ONL-002.
- Relabeling `$755–$1,774` as a dental-onlay-only range.
- Using the older `$350–$1,500` secondary figure as the primary benchmark without stronger methodology.
- One universal onlay price across ceramic, metal and resin materials.
- A universal insurance percentage or member copay.
- Using named-plan repair copays as new-onlay prices.
- Any assumption that exam, X-ray, temporary restoration, laboratory work, cementation, repair/recementing or other services are included in a quoted onlay price.
- Any recommendation that an onlay is preferable to an inlay, filling or crown for a particular reader.

## 7. First-version page direction

DEN-013 should be an **evidence-led cost guide, not a calculator**.

Recommended answer-first framing:
- Lead with the evidence limitation, not a fabricated onlay-only range.
- State the current combined ceramic inlay/onlay national reference accurately.
- Explain what makes onlay ownership distinct: its restoration definition and quote/service categories, especially material and surface count.
- Keep inlay pricing/details on DEN-004, filling pricing on DEN-010, and crown pricing on DEN-022.
- Keep repair separate from a new onlay.

Suggested lead:
> Current U.S. cost research does not provide a clean onlay-only national range. The strongest recent study reports a **$976 average and $755–$1,774 range for a single ceramic inlay or onlay combined**. Because that source groups the two restorations together, this page will not relabel those figures as an onlay-only price.

This evidence packet is sufficient to draft DEN-013 conservatively. A stronger current onlay-only national dataset would improve the page later but is not required for a transparent first version.
