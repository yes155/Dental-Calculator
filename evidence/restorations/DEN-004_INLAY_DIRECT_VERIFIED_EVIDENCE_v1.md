## 1. Evidence rules for DEN-004

1. DEN-004 owns dental inlay cost. DEN-013 owns dental onlay cost. Do not collapse the two URLs because a source reports them together.
2. Preserve the exact priced unit: inlay vs. onlay, material, surface count, geography, and whether the figure is a market observation or plan-specific member cost.
3. Do not relabel a combined `ceramic inlay or onlay` price as an inlay-only national benchmark.
4. Do not convert plan copays, plan allowances, local fee schedules, or one provider's price into a U.S.-wide cash-price estimate.
5. No material recommendation, treatment selection, diagnosis, eligibility decision, or claim that a reader needs an inlay rather than a filling/onlay/crown.
6. Insurance coverage is plan-specific. Do not apply a default coverage percentage, deductible, annual maximum, frequency limit, alternate benefit, or network rule.
7. If a source does not state whether an exam, X-ray, temporary restoration, lab charge, recementing, repair, or another service is included, treat inclusion as unknown.

## 2. Primary price evidence

### INL-001 — CareCredit / Synchrony: 7 Types of Dental Fillings: Pros, Cons and Costs
URL: https://www.carecredit.com/well-u/health-wellness/dental-filling-types/  
Article date observed: 2024-11-08  
Geography: 50 U.S. states + District of Columbia  
Research lineage: 2024 Synchrony Average Procedural Cost Study conducted by ASQ360° Market Research  
Price type: publisher-reported national procedural-cost observation; not a provider quote, guaranteed cash price, or insurer allowed amount

Verified observation relevant to DEN-004:
- Single ceramic **inlay or onlay**: **$976 average**.
- Reported combined range: **$755–$1,774**.

Critical scope restriction:
- The source explicitly combines ceramic inlays and onlays in one priced category.
- Therefore DEN-004 must **not** state that `$976` or `$755–$1,774` is an inlay-only national average/range.
- This figure may be used only as clearly labeled U.S. context for a **combined ceramic inlay/onlay category**, with DEN-013 linked as the separate onlay owner.

Publication-safe wording:
> A current U.S. cost study reports a $976 average and $755–$1,774 range for a single ceramic **inlay or onlay**. The source does not separate the two restorations, so those figures should not be treated as an inlay-only national range.

## 3. Secondary / historical inlay-only figure — not approved as primary benchmark

### INL-003 — CareCredit: Dental Inlays vs. Onlays: Key Differences Explained
URL: https://www.carecredit.com/well-u/health-wellness/dental-inlay-vs-onlay/  
Article date observed: 2023-07-21  

Observed statement:
- Dental inlays: **$250–$1,500**.
- Dental onlays: **$350–$1,500**.

Source-lineage limitation:
- CareCredit attributes these figures to a 2019 Consumer Guide to Dentistry article rather than to its later Synchrony/ASQ360 national procedural-cost study.
- The page does not provide the methodological detail needed to treat `$250–$1,500` as the site's primary current U.S. inlay benchmark.

Decision:
- **Do not use `$250–$1,500` as the lead national price range for DEN-004.**
- It may be retained in the evidence record as older secondary context only if explicitly labeled as such; first-version publication should prefer the newer combined ceramic inlay/onlay study and state its scope limitation.

## 4. Clinical terminology / scope evidence

### INL-004 — ADA MouthHealthy: Fillings (Gold Inlays)
URL: https://www.mouthhealthy.org/all-topics-a-z/fillings-gold-inlays  
Publisher: American Dental Association consumer education  
Use: terminology/material context only; no price benchmark

Verified scope usable for cost education:
- Gold fillings may be called inlays or onlays.
- They are made from a gold alloy with other metals.
- Gold inlays generally require more than one office visit because impressions are used to manufacture the restoration in a dental laboratory.

Safety boundary:
- Do not infer that gold is appropriate for a particular reader or recommend it over ceramic/resin alternatives.

### INL-005 — ADA MouthHealthy: Dental Filling Options
URL: https://www.mouthhealthy.org/all-topics-a-z/dental-filling-options  
Publisher: American Dental Association consumer education  
Use: material decision boundary only

Verified scope usable here:
- Material selection is individualized and should be discussed with the dentist.
- Cost and insurance/out-of-pocket considerations can be among the factors considered.

Safety boundary:
- DEN-004 may explain material labels on a quote but must not choose a material for the reader.

## 6. DEN-004 approved evidence conclusions

SUPPORTED:
- Inlay and onlay are separate restoration labels for site architecture even when some cost sources combine them.
- A current national CareCredit/Synchrony study supports **$976 average / $755–$1,774 range for a single ceramic inlay or onlay combined**.
- Material and surface count are legitimate quote/service descriptors.
- Gold inlays may require laboratory fabrication and more than one visit.
- Insurance handling can depend on plan-specific deductible, network, coinsurance/copay, annual maximum, frequency limits and alternate-benefit rules.

UNSUPPORTED / BLOCKED:
- A current high-confidence **inlay-only** national range derived from INL-001/INL-002.
- Relabeling `$755–$1,774` as a dental-inlay-only range.
- Using the older `$250–$1,500` secondary figure as the primary benchmark without stronger methodology.
- One universal inlay price across ceramic, metal and resin materials.
- A universal insurance percentage or member copay.
- Any assumption that exam, X-ray, temporary restoration, lab work, cementation, repair, recementing or other services are included in a quoted inlay price.
- Any recommendation that an inlay is preferable to a filling, onlay or crown for a particular reader.

## 7. First-version page direction

DEN-004 should be an **evidence-led cost guide, not a calculator**.

Recommended answer-first framing:
- Lead with the evidence limitation, not a fabricated inlay-only range.
- State the current combined ceramic inlay/onlay national reference accurately.
- Explain that a written inlay quote should identify material, surface count, what is included, and insurance basis.
- Keep onlay pricing/details on DEN-013 and filling/crown pricing on DEN-010/DEN-022.

Suggested lead:
> Current U.S. cost research does not give us a clean inlay-only national range. The strongest recent study reports a **$976 average and $755–$1,774 range for a single ceramic inlay or onlay combined**. Because that source groups the two restorations together, this page will not relabel those figures as an inlay-only price.

This evidence packet is sufficient to draft DEN-004 conservatively. A stronger current inlay-only national dataset would improve the page later but is not required to publish a transparent first version.
