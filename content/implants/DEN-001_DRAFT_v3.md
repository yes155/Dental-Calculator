# Dental Implant Cost Calculator: Single-Tooth Quote Components

CareCredit reports a U.S. national average of **$2,143** and a reported range of **$1,646–$4,157** for a full-size single-tooth implant benchmark. Its implant cost guide says this figure covers the process and material for implanting the artificial root and **does not include the dental crown**; state-level values also exclude tooth extraction and office-related fees. The figures come from Synchrony procedural-cost research covering all 50 states and the District of Columbia. They are published reference values, not a provider quote.

Research and written by Farrukh Abdullah.  
Evidence checked September 16, 2026.

## How much does a dental implant cost?

For the artificial-root implant process/material, CareCredit reports a **$2,143 national average** and a **$1,646–$4,157 range**. The final crown is excluded from that benchmark, so it should not be presented as the price of a fully restored tooth. Extraction and some office-related fees are also excluded from CareCredit’s state-level implant values.

A separate clean national default for a complete implant + abutment + crown package is not established by the current evidence set. Compare a provider’s written quote by its actual components instead of adding unsupported default fees.

Use CALC-001 with a written dental quote. It adds only the amounts you enter, keeps quote inclusions visible, and subtracts an insurer estimate only when you already have one for the same scope. It does not choose treatment, infer how many implants you need, or insert hidden market prices.

## What does a single-tooth implant quote include?

A written implant quote may list the implant body/process, abutment and final crown or prosthesis as separate components. FDA guidance distinguishes the implant body placed in the jaw from the abutment that supports the artificial tooth above it.

Your quote may also show extraction, bone grafting, imaging/exam, sedation/anesthesia or another adjunct as included, separately quoted, absent from the quote, or still unknown. “Not on the quote” describes the document only; it does not determine whether a service is clinically necessary.

## Implant post, abutment and crown costs are different quote components

An “implant cost” label does not always describe a complete restored tooth. CareCredit’s main single-tooth benchmark applies to the artificial-root implantation process/material and excludes the crown. A provider may bundle the abutment and crown or list them separately.

For that reason, this page does not automatically add a crown, abutment or other service to the published implant benchmark. The calculator uses only amounts entered from the written quote.

## What changes the price of a dental implant quote?

Quote totals can differ because of geography, provider pricing, the component scope, and separately quoted services. CareCredit reports **$3,255** as a separate average for its immediate-load implant category; that figure is not merged into the standard single-tooth range.

Humana also publishes a local implant example of **$856–$2,122 in Orlando, Florida**. That is a local pricing example, not a national benchmark, and it should not be blended with CareCredit’s nationwide study.

FDA guidance notes that implant systems may use materials such as titanium or zirconium oxide. A zirconia implant body is not the same component as a zirconia crown, so material labels should stay attached to the specific part of the quote they describe.

## How insurance can change out-of-pocket cost

There is no universal fixed implant coverage percentage. Dental benefits vary by plan, and patient responsibility can be affected by deductibles, waiting periods, exclusions, annual or lifetime maximums, network/allowed-amount rules, and the exact services on the treatment plan.

The calculator subtracts an insurer amount only when you enter an estimate that applies to the same quote scope. Verify benefits with the insurer or plan administrator before treatment. See the **dental insurance and out-of-pocket cost guide** (`/dental-insurance-out-of-pocket-costs/`) for the general mechanics.

## Mini, zirconia and immediate-load implant quote labels

Different labels may refer to implant size, material or timing rather than equivalent procedures. The current source set supports a separate CareCredit immediate-load average, but it does not provide a clean national default price for mini implants or zirconia implant bodies.

Those unsupported categories are therefore not auto-filled by the calculator, and this page does not recommend one implant material or system over another.

## When extraction or bone graft fees are separate

Extraction and grafting can materially change a written treatment estimate when they are billed separately. Do not assume that the implant benchmark includes either service.
