# Dental Bone Graft Cost by Graft Type and Quote Scope

CareCredit’s 2023–2024 U.S. procedural-cost research reports different average-cost ranges by graft type: **allograft $652–$1,575**, **alloplast $576–$1,375**, **autograft $2,161–$5,148**, and **xenograft $549–$1,386**. The research covers all 50 states and the District of Columbia. These are published per-graft reference ranges, not provider quotes, and the four categories should not be collapsed into one interchangeable price.

Research and written by Farrukh Abdullah.  
Evidence checked September 16, 2026.

## How much does a dental bone graft cost?

Dental bone graft costs vary substantially by graft type. CareCredit reports the following average-cost ranges per graft:

| Graft type | Published range |
| --- | ---: |
| Allograft | $652–$1,575 |
| Alloplast | $576–$1,375 |
| Autograft | $2,161–$5,148 |
| Xenograft | $549–$1,386 |

The source’s overall observed span runs from $549 to $5,148, but that should not be presented as though every graft type shares one common range. Material category and quote scope need to stay visible.

## Bone graft prices depend on the graft type and quoted site

CareCredit reports different price ranges by graft type and notes that procedure complexity, provider pricing and geography can affect cost. A written estimate should also make clear whether the graft is quoted per site, per procedure, or as part of another treatment package.

If the unit or inclusion status is unclear, treat it as unknown rather than normalizing it into a different fee structure.

## Autograft, allograft, xenograft and alloplast are different quote categories

The source categories refer to different graft materials: an autograft uses the patient’s own bone, an allograft uses human donor bone, a xenograft uses animal donor material, and an alloplast uses synthetic material.

Those definitions help interpret a written quote; they do not determine which material a reader needs or which option is clinically preferable. Material selection requires a dental professional’s evaluation.

## Is the bone graft included in an implant or extraction quote?

Do not assume that an implant or extraction price includes grafting. The implant evidence used on this site treats grafting as a potentially separate cost component, and a provider may bundle it, quote it separately, omit it from the current estimate, or leave the inclusion unclear.

If the written quote does not say, verify the scope before comparing totals. The **dental implant cost calculator** (`/dental-implant-cost-calculator/`) and **tooth extraction cost guide** (`/tooth-extraction-cost/`) keep those parent procedure costs separate from graft pricing.

## What can change the bone graft quote?

Published cost can vary with graft type, amount of reconstruction, provider pricing, geography and other case-specific quote details. Related services may also be itemized separately.

A local clinic fee should remain a local example; it should not be converted into a U.S.-wide estimate. Likewise, this page does not merge unlike graft categories into a synthetic national range.

## How insurance can affect the patient cost

Coverage varies by plan. Deductibles, waiting periods, exclusions, annual or lifetime maximums, network status and allowed amounts can change patient responsibility.

This page does not assume a fixed insurance percentage for grafting. Verify plan-specific benefits with the insurer or plan administrator, and use the **dental insurance and out-of-pocket cost guide** (`/dental-insurance-out-of-pocket-costs/`) for general benefit mechanics.

## What this page does not price or decide

This page does not determine whether you need a graft, which graft material is suitable, or whether sinus augmentation is indicated. **Sinus-lift pricing is intentionally excluded from DEN-007**, even if another source reports a sinus-lift fee.

Clinical decisions about graft need, timing and material require an individualized dental evaluation.
