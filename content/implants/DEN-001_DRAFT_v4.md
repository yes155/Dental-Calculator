# Dental Implant Cost Calculator

CareCredit reports a U.S. national average of **$2,143** and a reported range of **$1,646–$4,157** for a full-size single-tooth implant. Its guide says this price covers the process and material for placing the artificial root, but **does not include the crown**. State-level values also leave out tooth extraction and some office-related fees. The figures come from Synchrony procedural-cost research covering all 50 states and the District of Columbia. They are published reference values, not a provider quote.

Research and written by Farrukh Abdullah.  
Evidence checked September 16, 2026.

## How much does a dental implant cost?

CareCredit reports a **$2,143 national average** and a **$1,646–$4,157 range** for the implant itself — the artificial root placed in the jaw. The final crown is not included in that benchmark. Its state-level implant values also leave out tooth extraction and some office-related fees.

That means the published implant number is not the price of a complete replacement tooth. Your written estimate may also list a connector (abutment), crown, tooth removal, bone graft, X-rays or scans, sedation, or other charges. Use your dentist's quote to see what is actually included.

## Dental implant cost calculator

Have your dentist's written estimate handy. Enter the amounts exactly as shown. The calculator adds them up, shows what is included, and can subtract an insurance estimate if you already have one for the same quote. It does not guess treatment prices or insurance coverage.

The guided flow is: **Your quote → What's included → Insurance & result**. If your estimate already lists separate charges, the tool uses those entered lines rather than asking you to describe a bundled total.

## What does a single-tooth implant quote include?

The FDA describes an implant system as an implant body placed in the jaw plus an abutment — the connector that supports the replacement tooth. Your dentist may list the implant placement, connector and final crown as separate charges.

The quote may also mention tooth removal, bone grafting, X-rays or scans, sedation/anesthesia, or another charge. If the quote does not make one of these clear, the calculator keeps it as **Not sure** rather than assuming that it is included or excluded.

## Why the implant, connector and crown can be separate charges

An “implant cost” does not always mean the price of a complete replacement tooth. CareCredit's main single-tooth benchmark covers the implant/artificial-root process and does not include the crown. A dentist may include the connector and crown in one total or list them separately.

For that reason, this page does not automatically add a crown, connector or other service to the published implant benchmark. The calculator only uses amounts you enter from the written quote.

## What changes the price of a dental implant quote?

Prices can differ by location, dentist, what the quote includes, and whether other services are charged separately. CareCredit lists **$3,255** as a separate average for its immediate-load implant category; that figure is not mixed into the standard single-tooth range.

Humana publishes a local implant example of **$856–$2,122 in Orlando, Florida**. That is a local example, not a national benchmark, so it is not blended with CareCredit's nationwide study.

The FDA notes that implant systems may use materials such as titanium or zirconium oxide. A zirconia implant body is not the same part as a zirconia crown, so check which part of the quote the material name refers to.

## How insurance can change out-of-pocket cost

There is no single insurance percentage that applies to every implant. What a plan pays can depend on deductibles, waiting periods, exclusions, annual or lifetime maximums, network rules, allowed amounts, and the exact services on the treatment plan.

The calculator only subtracts an insurance amount when you enter an estimate for the same charges. Check the final benefit details with your insurer or plan administrator. See the **dental insurance and out-of-pocket cost guide** (`/dental-insurance-out-of-pocket-costs/`) for the general mechanics.

## How mini, zirconia and immediate-load labels can affect the quote

These labels can describe implant size, material or timing, so they should not be treated as interchangeable price categories. The current source set supports a separate CareCredit immediate-load average, but it does not provide a clean national default price for mini implants or zirconia implant bodies.

The calculator therefore does not fill in prices for those categories and does not recommend one implant type or material over another.

## When tooth removal or bone grafting costs extra

Tooth removal and bone grafting can raise the written total when they are charged separately. Do not assume that the implant benchmark includes either service.
