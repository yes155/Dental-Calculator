# Dental insurance and out-of-pocket costs: how to read your estimate

Research and written by Farrukh Abdullah.

A coverage percentage alone does not tell you what you will pay. Check the fee used in the calculation, any remaining deductible, your cost-sharing terms and available benefits. Then ask your insurer and dental office to confirm the estimate for your plan and planned services. The sections below explain the terms; the worked example uses hypothetical figures.

## What determines your dental out-of-pocket cost?

Start with your written quote and benefit documents. Use them to answer these questions:

- Which services, units and included items does the quote cover?
- What are the billed charge and the insurer's allowed amount?
- Does the dentist participate in your exact plan's network?
- What deductible, coinsurance or copay applies?
- How much benefit remains, and which limits or exclusions apply?

This is a practical verification checklist, not a substitute for your plan documents. Ask the insurer to explain any field you cannot match to your quote.

## Billed fees, allowed amounts and network status

Keep the dentist's charge separate from the fee your plan uses to calculate benefits.

| Quote field | What to identify |
|---|---|
| Billed charge | The amount the dental office submits for the service. |
| Allowed amount | The amount the plan recognizes for that procedure. |
| Fee difference | Any gap between those amounts; ask whether it is waived or payable. |

For example, the **2026 Delta Dental FEDVIP plan** distinguishes its allowance from submitted charges. Its in-network terms restrict charges above the negotiated allowance, with an exception for additional treatment charges agreed to in writing. Out-of-network responsibility can include a difference above the allowance. These are terms of that named plan, not a promise about yours. [Delta Dental FEDVIP brochure, Section 3](https://www.opm.gov/healthcare-insurance/healthcare/plan-information/plans/BrochureJson?brochureNumber=02AP-05&year=2026).

## Deductibles, coinsurance and copays

A deductible is an amount you pay toward applicable covered services before benefits begin. In Delta Dental's educational example, the deductible is applied before percentage sharing. Some services may be exempt; check the amount still remaining and whether it applies to your quoted service. [Delta Dental deductible explanation](https://www.deltadental.com/protect-my-smile/dental-insurance-101/dental-insurance-deductibles/).

Coinsurance is a percentage; a copay is a fixed amount. Check whose percentage is shown and what fee it applies to. [FEDVIP definitions, Section 9](https://www.opm.gov/healthcare-insurance/healthcare/plan-information/plans/BrochureJson?brochureNumber=02AP-05&year=2026).

Do not treat a benefit percentage as a complete payment calculation. Confirm the deductible, limits and other conditions before applying it.

## Annual maximums, exclusions and waiting periods

An **annual benefit maximum limits insurer payments** for services subject to that limit. If it is exhausted, further payment for those services may stop until the next benefit period. Some plans treat diagnostic or preventive services differently, so exhaustion does not establish that every later expense is entirely yours. Check the remaining amount, exemptions and reset date. [Delta Dental annual maximum explanation](https://www.deltadental.com/protect-my-smile/dental-insurance-101/what-is-dental-insurance-annual-maximum/).

That benefit maximum is different from a patient out-of-pocket maximum; it does not itself cap your spending. [Delta Dental EOB terminology](https://www.deltadental.com/protect-my-smile/dental-insurance-101/explanation-of-benefits-eob/).

The named FEDVIP contract also applies exclusions and service-frequency limits. Check your own contract rather than assuming its rules match. [FEDVIP brochure, Sections 5 and 7](https://www.opm.gov/healthcare-insurance/healthcare/plan-information/plans/BrochureJson?brochureNumber=02AP-05&year=2026).

A waiting period can delay eligibility for certain benefits. Whether one applies, and for how long, depends on the plan. [Delta Dental waiting-period explanation](https://www.deltadental.com/protect-my-smile/dental-insurance-101/dental-insurance-waiting-period/).

## A worked example with hypothetical numbers

**Every dollar amount and percentage below is invented for education.** This is an unnamed service under an imaginary plan—not a procedure price, national average, FEDVIP example or calculator default.

Assume one person receives one covered service. The allowance is fixed; the deductible applies; there is no copay, exclusion, waiting-period or frequency denial, secondary insurer, or earlier patient payment. Premiums are outside this calculation. The service counts toward the stated benefit maximum.

In the baseline, the dentist bills **$1,000**, the allowed amount is **$800**, and the provider waives the **$200** difference. The remaining deductible is **$50**, the patient's coinsurance is **20%**, and **$1,000** of insurer benefit remains.

1. Subtract the deductible: $800 − $50 = **$750**.
2. Calculate the insurer's share: 80% × $750 = **$600**.
3. Calculate the patient's share: $50 + (20% × $750) = **$200**.
4. Reconcile the charge: $200 waived + $600 insurer + $200 patient = **$1,000**.

The variations below change one baseline assumption at a time.

| Item | Baseline | A: less benefit remaining | B: fee difference payable |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Billed charge | $1,000 | $1,000 | $1,000 |
| Allowed amount | $800 | $800 | $800 |
| Remaining deductible | $50 | $50 | $50 |
| Patient coinsurance | 20% | 20% | 20% |
| Remaining insurer benefit | $1,000 | $300 | $1,000 |
| Amount waived by provider | $200 | $200 | $0 |
| Insurer payment | $600 | $300 | $600 |
| **Patient responsibility** | **$200** | **$500** | **$400** |

**Variation A:** Only $300 of benefit remains. Under the assumed rules, this caps the insurer's calculated $600 payment at $300. The patient pays $800 − $300 = **$500**. Including the waived $200, the amounts reconcile to $1,000.

**Variation B:** A hypothetical out-of-network agreement makes the $200 fee difference payable. All other baseline assumptions—including the allowance, deductible, percentages and available benefit—stay the same. Patient responsibility becomes $200 + $200 = **$400**; with the insurer's $600, this reconciles to $1,000.

Real plans can change several variables together. These examples explain arithmetic under stated assumptions; they do not determine your coverage or final bill.

## What to check before treatment and after a claim

A pretreatment estimate and an EOB serve different purposes. In the named FEDVIP plan, the insurer's pretreatment estimate is nonbinding. [FEDVIP brochure, Section 3](https://www.opm.gov/healthcare-insurance/healthcare/plan-information/plans/BrochureJson?brochureNumber=02AP-05&year=2026).

An **Explanation of Benefits (EOB)** explains how a claim was processed; it is not the dental office's bill. [Delta Dental EOB explanation](https://www.deltadental.com/protect-my-smile/dental-insurance-101/explanation-of-benefits-eob/).

Before treatment, ask the office and insurer to check:

1. Service names or codes, units and included items.
2. Billed and allowed amounts, and any fee difference you would pay.
3. Participation in your exact plan's network.
4. Remaining deductible, patient percentage or copay, and remaining benefit.
5. Exclusions, service-frequency limits, waiting periods and eligibility dates.
6. The estimate's conditions and how changes could affect it.

After the claim, compare the EOB with the office bill and your payment receipts. Ask the office to reconcile differences before assuming an EOB amount is your remaining balance.

Without insurance, request a written cash quote for the same services and units. Do not assume a discount or substitute an insurer allowance for that quote. For other ways to access care, see [affordable dental care options](/affordable-dental-care/).
