# Dental X-ray costs by type

Dental X-ray prices differ by the type of image or series being quoted. CareCredit's January 31, 2025 article, citing 2024 Synchrony research across all 50 states and Washington, D.C., reports a **$65 average for bitewings**, **$200 for panoramic imaging**, **$226 for a full-mouth series**, and **$466 for cone-beam CT**. These are publisher-reported historical observations, not guaranteed cash prices, insurer allowed amounts or patient balances.

Research and written by Farrukh Abdullah.  
Evidence checked September 17, 2026.

## How much do dental X-rays cost?

There is no single useful price for every dental X-ray because bitewings, periapical images, full-mouth series, panoramic images, cephalometric images and cone-beam CT are different quoted services.

CareCredit's 2024 national research reports prices separately by named imaging type. Keep those units separate when comparing a published benchmark with a dental-office estimate.

## Dental X-ray costs by type

| X-ray type | Reported average | Reported range |
|---|---:|---:|
| Bitewing | $65 | $52–$120 |
| Periapical | $55 | $42–$102 |
| Full-mouth series | $226 | $175–$428 |
| Occlusal | $55 | $43–$103 |
| Panoramic | $200 | $157–$343 |
| Cephalometric | $141 | $110–$274 |
| Cone-beam CT | $466 | $361–$879 |

Source: CareCredit, *How Much Do Dental X-Rays Cost? Prices by Type and Location*, published January 31, 2025, citing a 2024 Synchrony study covering the 50 states and District of Columbia.

The source does not disclose whether these figures are billed charges, cash prices or contracted allowed amounts. It also does not disclose the sample size, weighting, most image counts or exactly what each fee includes. Do not combine the seven rows into one synthetic “dental X-ray range.”

## Bitewing, periapical and full-mouth series are different price units

CareCredit's **$52–$120** headline range applies to bitewings. It should not be presented as the range for every dental X-ray.

A full-mouth entry is explicitly described as a series, but the priced table does not establish the exact image count for that fee. The periapical row should not be treated as a verified per-image fee unless a provider quote explicitly defines its own unit that way.

## Panoramic, cephalometric and cone-beam CT costs

The same source reports a **$200 average / $157–$343 range for panoramic imaging**, **$141 / $110–$274 for cephalometric imaging**, and **$466 / $361–$879 for cone-beam CT**.

These are named imaging modalities, not interchangeable versions of one service. Compare a provider's written quote with the matching modality and stated scope.

## Are dental X-rays included in an exam or cleaning visit?

Do not assume imaging is automatically included in a dentist-visit or cleaning price. A written estimate should show whether the imaging fee is separate or bundled and which type is included.

Exam-led package pricing belongs on the dentist-visit cost page. Cleaning-led package pricing belongs on the dental-cleaning cost page. This page owns the X-ray type-level prices.

## What can change the price of dental X-rays?

The strongest supported price distinction in the current evidence is the **named imaging type**. The CareCredit source does not provide enough detail to quantify the effect of every other variable.

For quote comparison, identify the imaging type, the quoted unit or series, any stated image count, what is included, and whether the amount is described as a cash fee, billed charge or another fee basis. Geography may also matter, but this page does not manufacture location adjustments from incomplete data.

## How insurance and frequency limits can affect what you pay

Dental-plan rules can affect the patient's amount, but there is no universal X-ray coverage percentage or repeat-frequency rule.

A named 2026 Delta Dental FEDVIP plan, for example, contains plan-specific imaging frequency limits. That is evidence that such limits can exist, not a rule for every Delta Dental plan or every dental policy. Verify the exact imaging type, frequency rule and same-scope insurer estimate under the reader's own plan.

## How often you need X-rays is not a cost-calculator decision

ADA MouthHealthy says a dentist reviews the patient's history and examines the mouth to determine whether X-rays are needed, and notes that a patient may not need an X-ray at every dental visit.

This cost page does not determine whether imaging is needed, which type is appropriate, or when it should be repeated.
