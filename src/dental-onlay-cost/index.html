Dental onlay cost

Current U.S. cost research does not provide a clean onlay-only national range. The strongest recent study reports a $976 average and a $755–$1,774 range for a single ceramic inlay or onlay combined. How much does a dental onlay cost?

The strongest current U.S. evidence is a combined ceramic inlay/onlay benchmark rather than an onlay-only price. CareCredit/Synchrony reports the benchmark from a 2024 procedural-cost study conducted across all 50 states plus Washington, D.C.; the study does not publish separate national values for inlays and onlays.

Use the published benchmark below as broad context, not as a dentist's quote, guaranteed cash price or insurer allowed amount. A written onlay estimate still needs its material, surface count, quoted unit and included services checked before it can be compared with another estimate.

Single ceramic inlay or onlay$976 average; $755–$1,774 rangeCombined inlay/onlay category; not an onlay-only national range

Why the current national price data combines onlays and inlays

The strongest current national source uses one priced category for a single ceramic inlay or onlay. It does not publish separate national values for the two restorations in that study.

This page therefore keeps the source category intact instead of turning the combined $755–$1,774 range into an onlay-only range. Inlay pricing remains a separate page topic under the site's frozen architecture, even when a source groups the two procedures together.

What makes an onlay a distinct restoration?

The American Dental Association defines an onlay as a restoration made outside the mouth that covers one or more cusp tips and adjoining chewing surfaces without covering the tooth's entire external surface. That definition supports treating an onlay as a distinct restoration and cost topic.

This definition helps identify the service on a written estimate; it does not determine whether an onlay is appropriate for a particular tooth or person.

What should an onlay quote identify?

Before comparing two onlay quotes, confirm the material, surface count, quoted unit, and what the total includes. Current plan documentation distinguishes onlays by material and surface count, but those plan-specific member costs are not national procedure prices.

Quote fieldWhat to confirm
MaterialWhether the written quote says metallic, porcelain/ceramic, resin-based composite or another material.
Surface countWhether the service description states how many surfaces the onlay covers.
Quoted unitWhether the amount is for one restoration and which tooth or service line it applies to.
Included servicesWhether related services are included, separate, not listed or unclear.

Material on the quote

Material is a quote descriptor, not a treatment recommendation. ADA material guidance describes indirect-restoration materials that can include metallic alloys, porcelain/ceramic and resin-based composites. This page records the label needed to interpret price scope; it does not choose a material for the reader.

Surface count on the quote

A named 2026 Humana Dental FEDVIP plan lists metallic onlays as two-, three-, and four-or-more-surface services. Do not infer surface count from the price alone; use the service description on the written estimate.

Are laboratory and related service costs included in an onlay price?

The current price evidence does not establish a universal inclusion rule. A written estimate should make clear whether laboratory work, exam or imaging, temporary restoration, cementation and any other related service is included, billed separately, not listed or unclear.

Do not treat an unclear inclusion as zero, and do not assume that every office packages related services the same way.

What can change an onlay quote?

The strongest supported quote distinctions are material, surface count, provider or geographic pricing, and what the written estimate includes. The current evidence does not support a universal adjustment percentage for any of those factors.

That is why this page does not manufacture a material multiplier, surface-count multiplier or local-price adjustment from the combined national benchmark. Compare like-for-like written service descriptions instead.

Onlay repair is not the same price category as a new onlay

Current plan documentation lists onlay repair as a separate service category from placement of a new onlay. A repair copay or repair fee therefore should not be relabeled as the price of a new onlay.

If a written estimate says repair, recementation or replacement, compare it with the matching service category rather than treating those labels as interchangeable.

How insurance can affect what you pay

There is no universal insurance percentage or copay for an onlay. A plan's deductible, network rules, coinsurance or copay, annual maximum, frequency limits and alternate-benefit provisions can affect the patient amount.

The 2026 Humana Dental FEDVIP document used for service-category evidence applies only to members of that named plan. Its member copays are not U.S. onlay prices and are not used as cost benchmarks here. For the broader mechanics, see dental insurance and out-of-pocket costs, and verify plan-specific benefits with the insurer or plan administrator.
