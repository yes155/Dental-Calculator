Full-mouth dental implant cost: per-arch quote guide

Full-mouth and full-arch implant quotes are difficult to compare when one price covers a single arch and another covers both arches. This page keeps the unit and package scope explicit.

Research and written by Farrukh Abdullah

Evidence checked September 16, 2026

How much do full-mouth dental implants cost?

Forbes Advisor reports an American Dental Association-attributed figure of $20,000–$45,000 for a “mouthful of implants” without insurance. That is useful only as broad context: the source does not define an exact implant count, prosthesis type, one-arch versus two-arch scope, or a complete list of included services.

Because those details are not established, this site does not divide that range in half to manufacture a national per-arch price. The calculator below works only from a written quote and a user-confirmed arch count.

Full-arch and full-mouth quotes are not the same unit

An arch means either the upper or lower jaw. A full-mouth quote may cover both arches, but providers can also quote one arch at a time. A reliable comparison therefore starts by confirming whether the written amount covers one arch or two.

What does a full-arch implant quote include?

AAP guidance describes full-mouth implant treatment using implants, prosthetic teeth and abutments. A written quote may also list temporary and final prostheses, extractions, bone grafting, imaging, sedation or follow-up visits separately. If a component is not stated, this page treats its inclusion as unknown rather than assuming it is bundled.

Fixed and removable implant restorations can be quoted differently

Some implant restorations are fixed, while others are removable implant-supported restorations. This distinction can matter to quote structure, but this page does not decide which type is appropriate for a reader. Use the wording on the treatment quote and confirm clinical questions with the treating dental professional.

What changes a full-mouth implant quote?

Important quote differences include the number of arches, restoration type, what is included in the package, separately quoted site preparation, and geographic/provider variation. All-on-4-specific package pricing belongs on the dedicated All-on-4 page and is not substituted for generic full-arch pricing here.

How insurance can affect the patient estimate

Coverage depends on the plan. Deductibles, waiting periods, annual or lifetime maximums, exclusions and allowed-amount rules may affect patient responsibility. The calculator subtracts an insurer estimate only when you already have that estimate for the same quote scope.

When the quote is specifically All-on-4

All-on-4 is a named full-arch treatment concept with its own cost evidence and package questions. If your written quote specifically says All-on-4, use the All-on-4 dental implant cost guide rather than treating a generic full-mouth figure as equivalent.
