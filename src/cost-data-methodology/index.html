Methodology

Cost Data Methodology

A dental price is useful only when its procedure, unit, geography, date, price type and inclusion scope are understood. Dental Calculator keeps those attributes attached to the number.

Source priority

We prefer U.S. government and public-health sources, professional dental organizations, peer-reviewed research, universities and dental schools, major insurers and recognized cost datasets. Specialist or dental-practice sources can be used for bounded examples but are not promoted to national benchmarks without supporting methodology.

What we record for an important price

  • procedure or priced entity;
  • geographic scope;
  • date or research vintage;
  • priced unit, such as per tooth, quadrant, arch, visit or named bundle;
  • price type, such as published procedural observation, provider fee, plan copay or allowed amount;
  • known inclusions, exclusions and assumptions;
  • source and whether the value is published, derived or modeled.

We do not manufacture ranges

Dental Calculator does not merge unrelated local fees, plan copays or differently scoped sources merely to create a national range. A bundled price stays a bundled price. A per-quadrant price is not multiplied into a default full-mouth total unless the evidence and user inputs support that calculation.

Local and insurance examples

A clinic or city-specific fee remains local. A named insurance-plan copay remains a plan-specific member amount. Neither becomes a U.S.-wide procedure price just because it is a convenient number.

Evidence gaps

When the available sources do not support a publication claim, the claim is held from publication until appropriate evidence is added. A page may instead publish a transparent limitation and explain how to interpret a written quote.

State-by-state price comparisons

Our state comparison tool uses published state averages only when one source provides the same named procedure across all 50 states and the District of Columbia with a clear unit and study attribution. The current geographic dataset uses CareCredit/Synchrony procedural-cost research conducted by ASQ360°. We keep each procedure separate and show the source scope beside the comparison.

We do not blend these state averages with Medicaid fee schedules, insurer allowed amounts, local clinic prices or another procedure type. Medicaid fee schedules are reimbursement rates, not consumer cash prices. FAIR Health and Delta Dental provide valuable local-cost methodology and location-based estimators, but we do not scrape or republish their interactive estimates as our own dataset.

The map is a visual comparison of published averages, not a quote, an insurance estimate or a prediction of what a dentist in a specific ZIP code will charge. A state average can hide large differences between cities, suburbs and rural areas.

What is not in the state tool yet

Standard cleaning is excluded because the available CareCredit state table has a bundle/scope conflict with our standalone cleaning benchmark. Root canal is also excluded from the geographic tool while we resolve inconsistent state values observed across source presentations. We would rather omit a procedure than publish an unstable comparison.

State pages and local estimates

The state selector is supplementary to the existing procedure pages. It does not create state-specific indexable URLs. A future state page would require separate search-intent validation, Page Registry approval, a unique promise and evidence sufficient to support that state page without templated filler.

Freshness

High-change price and insurance evidence is reviewed on its assigned schedule, commonly quarterly, and when a material source changes or a correction is received. The page’s evidence-check date identifies the review point; it is not a price guarantee.
