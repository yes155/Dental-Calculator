How much does a dental implant cost?

CareCredit reports a $2,143 national average and a $1,646–$4,157 range for the implant itself — the artificial root placed in the jaw. The final crown is not included in that benchmark. Its state-level implant values also leave out tooth extraction and some office-related fees.

That means the published implant number is not the price of a complete replacement tooth. Your written estimate may also list a connector (abutment), crown, tooth removal, bone graft, X-rays or scans, sedation, or other charges. Use the calculator below to organize what your dentist actually listed.

What does a single-tooth implant quote include?

The FDA describes an implant system as an implant body placed in the jaw plus an abutment — the connector that supports the replacement tooth. Your dentist may list the implant placement, connector and final crown as separate charges.

Part of the quoteWhat to check
Implant placement / artificial rootWhether the amount is for the implant and placement only
Connector (abutment)Whether it is included with the implant or charged separately
Final crownWhether it is included; the CareCredit implant reference above excludes the crown
Tooth removal, bone graft, X-rays/scans or sedationWhether each item is included, separate, not listed, or unclear

“Not listed” only means the item does not appear on the written quote. It does not tell you whether a dentist thinks that service is needed.

Why the implant, connector and crown can be separate charges

An “implant cost” does not always mean the price of a complete replacement tooth. CareCredit's main single-tooth benchmark covers the implant/artificial-root process and does not include the crown. A dentist may include the connector and crown in one total or list them separately.

For that reason, this page does not automatically add a crown, connector or other service to the published implant benchmark. The calculator only uses amounts you enter from the written quote.

What changes the price of a dental implant quote?

Prices can differ by location, dentist, what the quote includes, and whether other services are charged separately. CareCredit lists $3,255 as a separate average for its immediate-load implant category; that figure is not mixed into the standard single-tooth range.

Humana publishes a local implant example of $856–$2,122 in Orlando, Florida. That is a local example, not a national benchmark, so we do not blend it with CareCredit's nationwide study.

The FDA notes that implant systems may use materials such as titanium or zirconium oxide. A zirconia implant body is not the same part as a zirconia crown, so check which part of the quote the material name refers to.

How insurance can change out-of-pocket cost

There is no single insurance percentage that applies to every implant. What a plan pays can depend on deductibles, waiting periods, exclusions, annual or lifetime maximums, network rules, allowed amounts, and the exact services on the treatment plan.

The calculator only subtracts an insurance amount when you enter an estimate for the same charges. Check the final benefit details with your insurer or plan administrator.

How mini, zirconia and immediate-load labels can affect the quote

These labels can describe implant size, material or timing, so they should not be treated as interchangeable price categories. The current source set supports a separate CareCredit immediate-load average, but it does not provide a clean national default price for mini implants or zirconia implant bodies. The calculator therefore does not fill in prices for those categories or recommend one implant type over another.

When tooth removal or bone grafting costs extra

Tooth removal and bone grafting can raise the written total when they are charged separately. Do not assume that the implant benchmark includes either service. If an item is not listed on the quote, that only describes the document; it does not mean the service is unnecessary.
