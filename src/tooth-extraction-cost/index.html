How much does a tooth extraction cost?

Current published U.S. benchmarks put a simple tooth extraction at an average of $177 and a surgical tooth extraction at an average of $363. CareCredit is the source for those 2024 study averages. Humana lists Orlando, Florida examples of $225 for an erupted tooth or exposed root and $325 when an erupted tooth requires bone removal and/or sectioning. These are separate publisher-reported categories with different scopes. Do not merge them into one range or multiply them to estimate a total bill.

Simple vs. surgical extraction price labels

ADA MouthHealthy describes an extraction as having a tooth removed. Humana's consumer guide describes a simple extraction as one involving an accessible or visible tooth. It describes a surgical extraction as one used when a tooth cannot be easily accessed or is broken below the gumline. See Humana, “How Much Does a Tooth Extraction Cost?”.

Those descriptions explain Humana's labels; they do not classify a particular tooth or quote. Only a licensed dental professional can determine the procedure. This page and its calculator do not select a simple or surgical category.

What may be separate in an extraction quote?

The published price observations above do not disclose whether imaging, sedation or anesthesia, graft services, or other adjuncts are included. A written quote should make the status of each relevant line explicit.

Ask the dental office to mark every component as:

  • Separately billed.
  • Included in the named extraction bundle.
  • Not on the quote.
  • Unknown.

Do not treat an unknown inclusion as zero. This checklist does not mean that every component is needed or separately charged; it records what the quote actually says.

How insurance can affect the cost

This page does not predict whether an extraction or related quote line will be covered. If an insurer-payment estimate is available, confirm that it refers to the same procedure category, tooth or explicitly grouped set, and adjunct lines as the provider quote. If its amount or scope is unknown, a complete patient-share result should not be shown.

For general plan mechanics, see dental insurance and out-of-pocket costs. Verify any estimate with the dental office and insurer.

Enter either one complete, named extraction bundle or separately itemized provider-quoted extraction line totals. Do not enter both. Retain the provider's category label, tooth identifier or explicitly grouped tooth count, and line total exactly as shown on the quote. The calculator never chooses a simple or surgical treatment, infers a tooth count, or multiplies a published typical price.

For imaging, sedation or anesthesia, graft, and other adjunct lines, select separately billed, included in the bundle, not on the quote or unknown. An unknown scope or inclusion status makes the result incomplete.

Insurance starts unselected. A same-scope insurer-payment estimate may be entered. If the insurance amount or scope is unknown, the calculator suppresses the complete patient-share result. Wisdom-tooth labels are outside this calculator.

The tool totals quote amounts supplied by the user. It does not diagnose, determine urgency, recommend treatment, supply prices or recalculate benefits. See the calculator methodology.

How to compare extraction quotes

  1. Check the provider's exact procedure-category label.
  2. Check the tooth identifier or explicitly grouped tooth count.
  3. Confirm whether the amount is one named bundle or a set of itemized line totals.
  4. Mark whether imaging, sedation or anesthesia, quoted graft lines, and other adjuncts are included, separate, absent or unknown.
  5. Check the stated fee basis and any contractual adjustment already reflected.
  6. Confirm that an insurer estimate covers the same procedure and line-item scope.

Do not compare totals until their categories, units, scope and inclusions are clear. See the cost data methodology.
