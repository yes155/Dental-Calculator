How much does a dental filling cost?

Published U.S. filling prices vary significantly by material. See the table below for the full breakdown by type from CareCredit's 2024 Synchrony/ASQ360° research across the 50 states and District of Columbia.

For how published dental price benchmarks are selected and scoped, see our cost data methodology.

Without insurance: use the material-specific published figures as reference context rather than treating them as guaranteed cash fees. The amount on a provider quote can still vary with material, surfaces, tooth location and separately billed services.

These figures are publisher-reported observations, not guaranteed office charges or insurer allowed amounts. Do not treat the lowest value from one material and the highest value from another as a single standard-filling range.

CareCredit also reports a $226 national average for composite. Keep each material label attached to its own price data; the endpoints across different materials are not one generic filling range.

How surface count and tooth location can change a quote

Humana's Orlando, Florida examples show that quoted filling amounts can differ by material, surface count and anterior/posterior location. Examples include composite one-surface anterior $199, one-surface posterior $217, two-surface posterior $274, three-surface posterior $333, and a two-surface amalgam example of $229.

These figures are local examples only. The calculator records surface count and location from the written quote as descriptive labels; it never adjusts a price automatically from those fields.

What may be separate in a filling quote?

The verified national material ranges do not establish that exam fees, imaging, anesthesia or other adjuncts are included. If a provider lists any of those separately, enter them as separate charges. If the quote does not say, leave the item as “Not sure.”

What changes a dental filling quote?

The current evidence supports keeping material, surface count, tooth location and restoration count visible when the written estimate provides them. The page does not use those labels to choose a material or determine clinical treatment.

How insurance can affect the patient amount

Dental-plan rules vary by exact service and plan. Deductibles, allowed amounts, coinsurance, network status, exclusions, waiting periods and annual maximums may affect the patient amount. The calculator does not assume any of them.

Only a user-entered insurer estimate confirmed for the same written quote can be subtracted.

Fillings, bonding, inlays and onlays are different quote categories

This page covers filling/restoration pricing. Cosmetic bonding belongs on the planned dental bonding cost page. Inlay and onlay pricing belongs on dental inlay cost and dental onlay cost. Root-canal treatment belongs on the root canal cost page.

The presence of porcelain/ceramic in a filling price source does not authorize this page to choose an inlay, onlay or other restoration for a reader.
