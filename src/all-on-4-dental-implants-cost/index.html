All-on-4 dental implant cost: per-arch quote guide

All-on-4 quotes can be difficult to compare when arch count and package inclusions are unclear. This page separates the published reference price from your own written quote and makes the scope visible.

Research and written by Farrukh Abdullah

Evidence checked September 16, 2026

How much do All-on-4 dental implants cost?

CareCredit reports a national average of $15,176 and a reported range of $11,640–$27,500 for All-on-4 dental implants, based on a 2024 Synchrony Average Procedural Cost Study conducted by ASQ360° across all 50 states plus Washington, D.C.

The article describes All-on-4 as replacing all teeth in either the upper or lower jaw with a bridge supported by four implants. However, the price sentence itself does not independently state “per arch,” so this site does not automatically double the published range for two arches or assume it covers a full mouth.

What does All-on-4 mean in a dental quote?

All-on-4 is a Nobel Biocare trademark and full-arch treatment concept. Manufacturer material is used here only for naming and concept attribution, not for claims that the treatment is clinically superior, universally graft-free or appropriate for a particular reader.

Is an All-on-4 price per arch or for a full mouth?

A written quote should state whether it covers the upper arch, lower arch, or both. If the arch count is not clear, do not infer it from the price. The calculator below requires you to confirm the number of arches explicitly covered before it calculates a per-arch normalization.

What may be included in an All-on-4 package?

The published CareCredit procedure description discusses temporary and permanent bridges, but the price block does not establish that extraction, grafting, imaging, a temporary bridge, a final bridge and follow-up visits are all included. For quote comparison, each of those items should be marked included, separately quoted, not on the quote, or unknown.

What changes an All-on-4 quote?

Important differences include the number of arches, prosthesis/package scope, separately quoted extraction or grafting, imaging, sedation, follow-up services, provider/location and the materials specified by the written treatment plan. None of those factors should be inferred from price alone.

How insurance can affect out-of-pocket cost

Implant coverage is plan-specific. Deductibles, waiting periods, annual or lifetime maximums, exclusions and allowed-amount rules can change patient responsibility. The calculator accepts only an insurer estimate already supplied for the same package scope; it does not assume a percentage.

All-on-4 vs. generic full-arch implant quotes

All-on-4-specific evidence should not be substituted for every full-arch implant treatment. For generic full-mouth/full-arch quote normalization, use the full-mouth dental implant cost guide.
